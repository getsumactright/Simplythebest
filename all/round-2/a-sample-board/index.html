What’s on your wall?

A patch only disappears when the texture around it matches. Here’s what each finish is and how it gets put back.

  1. Smooth Level 5

    Where you see it
    Modern rooms, accent walls, anywhere you want a clean, flat surface.
    How it’s done
    A thin skim coat over the whole surface, sanded flat. There’s no pattern to hide a patch, so every pass has to be right.

  2. Imperfect smooth Ray’s bathroom retexture

    Where you see it
    Bathrooms, and rooms with an old-plaster feel.
    How it’s done
    Troweled by hand, leaving soft movement you only notice when light rakes across it.

  3. Orange peel

    Where you see it
    Walls in many newer homes.
    How it’s done
    Compound sprayed on in fine, even droplets. Matching it means getting the spray size and spacing right.

  4. Knockdown

    Where you see it
    Walls and ceilings in a lot of California homes.
    How it’s done
    Heavier splatter, flattened with a knife before it dries, leaving soft islands with smooth valleys between.

  5. Skip trowel

    Where you see it
    Living rooms and entries, especially Spanish and Mediterranean styles.
    How it’s done
    Compound skipped across the wall with a curved trowel. No two walls come out the same, so it’s matched by eye.

  6. Popcorn Removal & respray

    Where you see it
    Ceilings in older homes.
    How it’s done
    The room gets covered, the acoustic texture is scraped off, and the ceiling is resprayed with a new texture.
